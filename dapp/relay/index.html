The relay, in the open

The relay proves and settles what people send through it, and keeps Bitcoin’s state on Ethereum current for everyone. Here is what it earns, what that costs, and the tenth of its fee income that buys TAC. Below: settles it has proved that anyone can send, and how to pay for its proofs.

Where it goes

  1. Fees paid in ETH, or in a token the relay converts, land in the settle wallet. A tenth of them goes to the TAC buyback, every pass; the rest pays settle gas and proving.
  2. Fees paid in TAC are never sold: the relay moves them to the protocol reserve.
  3. A wrap’s tip pays for settling it.
  4. The buyback turns its ETH into TAC on the public market, a little at a time, and sends the TAC to the reserve.

Anyone can send these. Each is a settle the relay has proved; the pool’s settle is open to every account, and whoever sends one is paid the fee its proof binds, in the asset it names. Your wallet pays the network fee, and each is tried first, so one already sent is not sent again. To send them as they come, run a relayer.

Pay for proofsprover account

Every proof the relay makes is paid in PROVE, the token of Succinct’s prover network, from one prover account. PROVE sent to that account is put to work within a few minutes, or at the next half-hourly top-up when it is under 2 PROVE. About 0.4 PROVE pays for one proof.
